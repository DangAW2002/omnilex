## Easy explanation
If a sensor serves as the eyes and ears of an automated system, an actuator serves as its muscles: it receives instructions from a controller and executes a tangible physical movement, such as spinning a motor, opening a valve, or sliding a deadbolt.

## Details
Actuators translate low-energy electrical or algorithmic instructions into mechanical work. Depending on the energy medium utilized, actuators are categorized into electromechanical (stepper motors, solenoids, servos), pneumatic (compressed air pistons), and hydraulic (pressurized fluid cylinders). Key engineering metrics include stroke length, torque, slew speed, positioning accuracy, and holding force. Closed-loop control systems often integrate feedback sensors with actuators to verify that the commanded mechanical position is accurately achieved.

## Technical definition
A mechanical or electro-mechanical device that accepts an input control signal and transforms stored energy into controlled physical motion, torque, or mechanical displacement.

## Examples
A motorized ball valve actuator that rotates ninety degrees to shut off the water supply when an automated leak detector triggers an alarm.
