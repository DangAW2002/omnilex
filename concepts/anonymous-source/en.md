## Easy explanation
An anonymous source is like a whistleblower slipping a folder under a door: they provide crucial facts while their real name stays strictly hidden.

## Details
Investigative journalism depends on confidential informants to expose corruption, corporate malfeasance, or security failures when coming forward openly would bring retaliation. Editors mandate strict standards before granting anonymity: the information must be vital to the public interest, unobtainable on the record, and independently verified by secondary sources or physical documents. Reporters protect source identities even when facing subpoenas or imprisonment.

## Technical definition
An individual who provides confidential testimony or documentation to a journalist under a binding agreement that their identity will remain undisclosed in published reports.

## Examples
A senior municipal auditor secretly shares financial ledgers with an investigative journalist, allowing the paper to document public procurement fraud while citing only a confidential official.
