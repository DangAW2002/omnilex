## Easy explanation
B-roll is like the background b-side of a cassette: while the speaker talks about growing coffee beans, the video shows close-ups of harvesting red coffee cherries rather than staring at their talking face the entire time.

## Details
In video production and broadcast journalism, A-roll captures the primary talking head or central subject, while B-roll provides cutaway shots. Video editors drop B-roll footage onto timeline overlay tracks to mask jump cuts when trimming speech pauses or stumbles. Cinematographers intentionally shoot varied establishing shots, medium details, and extreme close-ups on location to give post-production editors ample visual rhythm options.

## Technical definition
Auxiliary visual footage captured to complement, cover, and contextualize primary narrative cinematography or interview sequences during editorial montage.

## Examples
In a documentary profile of an oceanographer, the editor cuts away from the seated interview to sweeping aerial drone footage of research vessels navigating arctic ice shelves.
