## Easy explanation
A bank transfer works like sending an email with digital cash attached: instead of carrying paper banknotes across town, your bank deducts the sum from your account and instructs the receiver's bank to credit their balance.

## Details
Transfers move through central interbank clearing systems such as Fedwire and CHIPS for high-value real-time gross settlement, the Automated Clearing House (ACH) for retail batch processing, or instant networks like FedNow and SEPA Instant. To initiate a transfer, the sender provides the recipient's name, account number, and bank routing code (such as an ABA routing number or IBAN). Processing times range from instant settlement within seconds to several business days for standard international wires, which rely on the SWIFT messaging network.

## Technical definition
An electronic debit-credit instruction executed across depository institutions to shift central bank reserve balances or commercial bank liabilities from an originating account to a beneficiary account.

## Examples
A small business owner initiates a $4,500 supplier payment through online banking, routing funds directly from her commercial checking account to the vendor's account via the national automated clearing network.
