## Easy explanation
Yield is the real-world mileage you get from your investment: while the bond coupon is the manufacturer's nominal rating, your actual yield depends on how much you paid for the bond in the secondary market.

## Details
An investor purchasing a bond at par ($1,000) with a 5% coupon earns a 5% yield. However, if market interest rates rise and the bond's price falls to $950 (discount), the new purchaser earns a higher yield because the fixed $50 annual coupon represents a larger percentage of the $950 outlay, plus capital appreciation at maturity. Key metrics include current yield (annual dollar coupon divided by market price) and yield to maturity (YTM), which solves for the internal rate of return assuming all future coupon payments are reinvested at the same rate until maturity.

## Technical definition
The annualized internal rate of return equating the present discounted value of all future contractual bond cash flows (coupons and par redemption) to its current market price.

## Examples
A bond with a $1,000 face value and a 6% annual coupon ($60) trades at a discounted price of $920; its current yield equals approximately 6.52% ($60 / $920).
