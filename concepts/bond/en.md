## Easy explanation
Buying a bond is like acting as the bank: you lend money to a government or corporation, they pay you regular interest payments as a thank-you fee, and when the loan term ends, they return your original principal.

## Details
When governments or corporations need funding for capital projects, they issue bonds with specified par values, coupon rates, and maturity dates. In secondary markets, bond prices fluctuate inversely with benchmark interest rates: when prevailing market rates rise, existing lower-yielding bonds trade at a discount; when rates fall, existing higher-paying bonds trade at a premium. Unlike equity holders, bondholders hold senior creditor claims during corporate insolvency and bankruptcy restructuring.

## Technical definition
A contractual negotiable debt obligation under which an issuer promises to disburse periodic coupon payments and redeem the par principal amount at a fixed terminal date.

## Examples
An investor buys a 10-year Treasury note with a $10,000 par value and a 4.0% coupon rate, collecting $400 annually in interest before receiving the $10,000 principal repayment at maturity.
