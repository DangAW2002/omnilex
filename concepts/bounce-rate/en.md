## Easy explanation
Imagine a shopper who opens the front door of a bookshop, glances briefly at the front desk for five seconds, turns right around, and walks straight back out onto the street without touching a single book.

## Details
Bounce rate historically quantified single-page sessions where a user entered and departed without triggering a secondary hit. In Google Analytics 4 (GA4), bounce rate represents the exact mathematical complement of engagement rate: the proportion of visits falling outside engaged thresholds (specifically visits shorter than 10 seconds, logging under two page views, or generating no conversion milestones). A high bounce rate on multi-step conversion funnels, checkout portals, or landing pages indicates critical friction, misleading ad promises, slow page loading, or poor device compatibility. Conversely, on informational articles or customer support lookup pages, a rapid exit may simply mean the visitor immediately found the exact phone number or answer they sought.

## Technical definition
The proportion of recorded web traffic sessions classified as unengaged, calculated in modern digital analytics as one hundred percent minus the measured engagement rate.

## Examples
An online appliance repair service launches a PPC campaign targeting emergency fixes; forty out of one hundred landing page visitors immediately press the back button within three seconds, registering a bounce rate of 40%.
