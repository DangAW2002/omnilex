## Easy explanation
Picture a countdown fuel gauge on an airplane flight: as flight hours tick away, the fuel meter should steadily descend toward zero right as the aircraft lands smoothly on the runway.

## Details
A burndown chart features time across the horizontal X-axis (days in a sprint) and outstanding work across the vertical Y-axis (story points or task hours). An "ideal line" slopes downward from the total initial commitment to zero at the sprint's conclusion. The "actual line" tracks genuine completion daily. When the actual line hovers above the ideal slope, the team is behind schedule and may need to renegotiate scope; when it dips below, work is running ahead of plan.

## Technical definition
A project tracking graphic that plots outstanding backlog effort as a function of elapsed time within a defined development iteration or release milestone.

## Examples
A team viewing their Day 7 burndown observes 25 story points remaining out of 30 committed, highlighting that blocked code reviews are preventing tasks from reaching the Definition of Done.
