## Easy explanation
A credit card is like an ongoing, short-term tab at your local store: the issuing bank vouches for you and pays the merchant today, and at the end of the monthly billing cycle, you settle the total bill.

## Details
Unlike debit cards which draw immediately from deposit accounts, credit cards provide revolving unsecured credit. Issuers grant an interest-free grace period (typically 21 to 25 days) where no finance charge accrues if the full statement balance is paid on time. If a balance rolls over, compound interest applies at the card's annual percentage rate (APR), which often exceeds 20%. Cards also provide statutory consumer protections against unauthorized transactions, fraud liability caps, and promotional rewards such as cash back or travel points.

## Technical definition
A revolving credit facility linked to a physical or digital payment instrument, authorizing the cardholder to draw on an unsecured credit line up to a contractually determined limit.

## Examples
A consumer purchases a $900 laptop using a credit card on October 1st and repays the full $900 before the due date on November 20th, incurring zero interest while earning 2% cash back.
