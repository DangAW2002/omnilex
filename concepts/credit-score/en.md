## Easy explanation
A credit score is like a school report card for borrowing money: if you turn in assignments on time and manage your workload responsibly, you get high marks that make lenders eager to trust you.

## Details
Credit bureaus compute this score using standardized models like FICO or VantageScore, typically ranging between 300 and 850. Major scoring components include payment history (35%), credit utilization ratio (30%), length of credit history (15%), credit mix (10%), and recent inquiries (10%). Higher scores unlock preferential terms, such as lower annual percentage rates (APR) on auto loans and mortgages, higher credit limits, and waiving security deposits on utilities.

## Technical definition
A statistically derived, standardized metric quantifying the probability that an obligor will experience a delinquency event or default on contractual credit obligations within a specified forecast horizon.

## Examples
Two people apply for the same 30-year mortgage. The one with a 780 score is typically offered a lower interest rate than the one with a 630 score, and over the life of the loan that gap can add up to tens of thousands of dollars in interest.
