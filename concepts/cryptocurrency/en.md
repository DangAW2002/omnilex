## Easy explanation
Cryptocurrency is digital money that exists entirely as computer code: instead of relying on a government or bank to print paper bills and track who owns what, a global computer network enforces mathematical rules so no one can spend money they do not have.

## Details
Unlike traditional fiat currencies issued by sovereign monetary authorities, cryptocurrencies operate on decentralized peer-to-peer networks secured by public-key cryptography. Users generate a private key to authorize outgoing transactions and a public address to receive incoming funds. New currency units are typically minted through protocol-defined economic incentives such as block validation rewards, often with a mathematically enforced issuance cap. While offering borderless censorship-resistant value transfer, cryptocurrencies experience high price volatility and present consumer protection challenges relating to irreversible transactions and custody loss.

## Technical definition
A digital medium of exchange operating on a distributed cryptographic ledger that regulates unit generation and verifies transactions using asymmetric cryptography without reliance on a central intermediary.

## Examples
Bitcoin (BTC) operates as a decentralized digital store of value with a hard-coded maximum supply of 21 million coins, whereas Ether (ETH) serves as the native currency powering computational gas transactions on the Ethereum blockchain.
