## Easy explanation
Think of a small bakery shop suddenly crammed with thousands of unruly rowdies who fill every doorway, ask endless silly questions, and block the cash register so that genuine hungry customers outside cannot enter to buy bread.

## Details
In a DDoS attack, adversaries commandeer a botnet—a swarm of thousands or millions of compromised consumer routers, IoT appliances, and servers. Attackers direct these devices to simultaneously send massive volumes of requests toward the victim's infrastructure. Attacks fall into three broad classes: volumetric floods (UDP or ICMP floods saturating internet uplinks), protocol attacks (SYN floods exhausting state tables in load balancers and firewalls), and application-layer attacks (HTTP GET/POST floods that force costly database queries). Mitigation involves anycast routing, cloud-scale scrubbing centers, adaptive rate limiting, and web challenge barriers.

## Technical definition
A malicious attempt to disrupt the normal traffic of a targeted server, service, or network by overwhelming the target or its surrounding infrastructure with a distributed flood of internet traffic.

## Examples
An online payment gateway faces an incoming barrage of 1.2 Terabits per second of spoofed UDP traffic generated by 80,000 compromised surveillance cameras, triggering upstream scrubbing filters.
