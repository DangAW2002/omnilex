## Easy explanation
The D/E ratio shows how heavily a business relies on borrowed money from banks compared to the money invested by its owners.

## Details
Expressed as total liabilities divided by shareholders’ equity, this ratio reveals financial leverage. Higher leverage magnifies profit per share when sales soar, but escalates debt servicing burdens during contractions. Lenders monitor D/E thresholds carefully to prevent excessive risk taking by corporate boards.

## Technical definition
A solvency and capital structure ratio evaluating relative proportions of creditor financing and owner equity financing utilized by an entity.

## Examples
A business with $20 million in outstanding bank loans and $10 million in equity has a D/E ratio of 2.0, meaning creditors have funded twice as much capital as owners.
