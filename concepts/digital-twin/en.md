## Easy explanation
A digital twin is a living 3D software replica of a physical machine running on a computer screen: as the real jet engine flies through clouds, its digital twin in the control center updates its virtual blades and temperatures in real time to show exactly how it is holding up.

## Details
A digital twin bridges physical and virtual realms by maintaining an up-to-date software state graph populated by live IoT telemetry, engineering CAD geometry, and maintenance logs. Standard modeling frameworks like the Digital Twins Definition Language (DTDL) represent topologies of spatial relationships, capabilities, and telemetry feeds. Beyond passive visualization, advanced digital twins utilize physics-based finite element analysis (FEA) and machine learning models to run what-if scenario simulations, predicting component fatigue and optimizing operational parameters.

## Technical definition
A dynamic, software-defined representation of a physical asset, process, or system that mirrors its real-world state, behavior, and lifecycle through synchronized data exchanges.

## Examples
A wind energy company running a digital twin of an offshore turbine farm, synchronizing live wind speed and rotor vibrations to simulate blade structural fatigue and optimize yaw angles.
