## Easy explanation
Diversification is the classic rule of not putting all your eggs into one basket: if you carry all ten eggs in a single carton and drop it, every egg breaks, but if you distribute them into ten separate bags, dropping one bag only loses a single egg.

## Details
By allocating capital across distinct asset classes (such as equities, government bonds, real estate, and cash reserves) and diverse economic sectors (technology, healthcare, consumer staples, manufacturing), an investor minimizes exposure to single-company failures. While diversification cannot protect against broad market-wide downturns (systematic risk), it effectively mitigates idiosyncratic risks unique to an individual enterprise or specific industry sector. Combining assets whose price movements exhibit low or negative statistical correlation creates smoother portfolio growth over market cycles.

## Technical definition
A portfolio management strategy that combines imperfectly correlated financial assets to minimize idiosyncratic variance without necessarily lowering anticipated portfolio expected returns.

## Examples
Rather than putting an entire retirement nest egg into the stock of their employer, an investor distributes savings across an S&P 500 index fund, a 10-year Treasury bond fund, and an international emerging markets fund.
