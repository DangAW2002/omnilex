## Easy explanation
A dividend is like receiving a harvest bonus from an apple orchard you helped plant: when the orchard yields a bumper crop and sells apples at a profit, the farm manager sends you a box of fresh fruit or cash as your share of the bounty.

## Details
When a corporation generates net profits, management and the board of directors decide whether to reinvest the capital as retained earnings or distribute a portion to equity holders. Dividends can be paid in cash or additional shares of stock. Key calendar milestones govern dividend rights: the declaration date (when the board announces the payout), the ex-dividend date (the cutoff date where purchasing shares no longer confers dividend rights), the record date (when eligible holders are registered), and the payment date. Established blue-chip enterprises with mature business models frequently prioritize regular quarterly dividends to attract income-seeking investors.

## Technical definition
A formal pro-rata distribution of retained earnings or capital reserves authorized by corporate governance to equity security holders of record on an ex-dividend basis.

## Examples
A public utility pays a $0.75 quarterly cash dividend per share; an investor holding 400 shares receives $300 directly in his brokerage account every three months, totaling $1,200 annually.
