## Easy explanation
An e-wallet is like carrying your traditional leather billfold on your smartphone screen: it securely holds your bank cards, transit passes, and prepaid cash balances so you can tap your phone at checkout without touching physical paper bills.

## Details
E-wallets operate either as stored-value platforms (like PayPal or Venmo) where users maintain a prepaid ledger balance, or as pass-through tokenized containers (like Apple Pay or Google Wallet) that securely store virtual tokens representing underlying credit or debit cards. Security is enhanced through tokenization—replacing the 16-digit card number with a cryptographic token—alongside biometrics such as fingerprint and facial recognition. Regulators frequently caution consumers that stored balances held outside FDIC-insured bank partnerships may face insolvency risks if the nonbank fintech provider defaults.

## Technical definition
A software-based client or server application that stores digitized payment tokens, credentials, and cryptographic keys to authenticate electronic transactions across point-of-sale terminals or online payment gateways.

## Examples
A commuter pays for a subway fare by tapping her smartphone against a contactless terminal, where the digital wallet transmits a one-time cryptographic token linked to her bank card in under a second.
