## Easy explanation
EBITDA looks at how much cash the core business engine generates on its own, stripping out loan interest, government taxes, and accounting write-downs on old machinery.

## Details
By taking operating profit and adding back non-cash depreciation and amortization, this non-GAAP indicator standardizes comparison across firms with disparate debt structures or tax environments. However, critics like Warren Buffett caution that ignoring continuous equipment replacement capital expenditures can give an inflated view of disposable earnings.

## Technical definition
A non-GAAP financial metric derived by calculating earnings before deducting interest expense, income taxes, depreciation of physical assets, and amortization of intangibles.

## Examples
A telecom provider with $100 million in operating profit adds back $40 million in network hardware depreciation and $10 million in spectrum amortization, yielding $150 million EBITDA.
