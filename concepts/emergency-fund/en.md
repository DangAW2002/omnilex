## Easy explanation
An emergency fund works like a spare tire in your trunk: you hope you never have to use it, but when a sudden flat occurs on the highway, it gets you safely to the repair shop without leaving you stranded.

## Details
Financial planners commonly recommend keeping three to six months of baseline living expenses in a secure, capital-preserving vehicle such as a high-yield savings account. It must remain liquid and strictly separated from daily checking accounts to prevent casual spending. The fund is reserved exclusively for true emergencies—such as medical crises, sudden job loss, or essential vehicle repairs—preventing the individual from carrying high-interest credit card debt or selling investments during market downturns.

## Technical definition
A dedicated pool of cash or near-cash liquid assets held to mitigate personal liquidity risk arising from unexpected stochastic expenditures or abrupt cessation of earned income.

## Examples
An engineer with monthly living expenses of $3,500 maintains $15,000 in a government-insured bank savings account, allowing her to pay rent and groceries uninterrupted during a three-month transition between employment contracts.
