## Easy explanation
An ETF is like buying a pre-packaged fruit basket at a grocery store counter: instead of selecting and purchasing apples, oranges, and bananas individually, you buy one single basket in one transaction with immediate delivery.

## Details
ETFs combine the diversification benefits of mutual funds with the liquidity and trading flexibility of common stocks. Shares are bought and sold through standard brokerage accounts with bid and ask prices updating every second throughout market hours. While many ETFs passively track benchmark indices like the S&P 500, actively managed ETFs also exist. Institutional market makers known as Authorized Participants (APs) create and redeem creation units in large blocks, an arbitrage process that continuously keeps market prices closely tethered to the fund's underlying net asset value. ETFs generally feature low expense ratios and favorable capital gains tax efficiency.

## Technical definition
A registered open-end investment company or unit investment trust whose listed shares trade intraday on organized securities exchanges at negotiated market prices reflecting underlying portfolio assets.

## Examples
An investor aiming for broad exposure to American large-cap equities purchases shares of the SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust (SPY) through their trading app, completing the transaction at 11:30 AM at the prevailing market quotation.
