## Easy explanation
Fact-checking is like an umpire reviewing slow-motion video: investigators examine public statements frame by frame to determine whether claims are true or false.

## Details
Professional fact-checking encompasses both internal copy verification before an article runs and public-facing forensic scrutiny of political speeches or viral internet memes. Fact-checkers consult primary archives, statistical databases, and recognized subject specialists rather than relying on hearsay. Non-partisan fact-checking organizations publish step-by-step methodologies and cite public sources so readers can independently retrace the verification chain.

## Technical definition
The systematic investigation of factual assertions in public discourse or media reports against authoritative documentary records and empirical data.

## Examples
A watchdog organization evaluates a politician's televised claim about unemployment rates, cross-referencing official labor bureau statistics to rule the statement partially false.
