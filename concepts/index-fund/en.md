## Easy explanation
An index fund is like copying the answers from the entire class average on an exam: instead of trying to guess which individual student will get the highest score, you automatically match the aggregate score of the whole group.

## Details
Instead of paying expensive portfolio managers to pick winners and losers, an index fund relies on algorithmic replication rules to purchase every asset in a designated benchmark—such as the S&P 500 or Russell 2000—weighted according to market capitalization or another predetermined methodology. Because transaction turnover is minimal and research overhead is virtually eliminated, index funds incur substantially lower management expense ratios than active funds. This systematic cost advantage, combined with broad market diversification, enables index funds to achieve superior risk-adjusted net returns over long multi-decade investment horizons.

## Technical definition
A portfolio management vehicle constructed to mirror the systematic risk, returns, and component weightings of a specified financial index through full replication or representative stratified sampling.

## Examples
An investor seeking exposure to the broader American corporate economy allocates capital into the Vanguard 500 Index Fund, which automatically buys shares in all 500 index constituent companies in proportion to their market valuations.
