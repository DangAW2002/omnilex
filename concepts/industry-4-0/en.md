## Easy explanation
Following the eras of steam engines, assembly-line electricity, and early computer automation, Industry 4.0 is the era of smart factories where machines talk to each other and reconfigure themselves on the fly to build custom products.

## Details
Originating as a strategic German industrial initiative, Industry 4.0 represents the holistic digitalization of manufacturing and industrial value chains. The paradigm rests on core technical pillars: Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS) that mirror physical operations in software, horizontal integration across global supplier networks, vertical integration connecting shop-floor sensors to enterprise ERP suites, and end-to-end digital engineering. Technologies including collaborative robotics, additive 3D manufacturing, digital twins, and autonomous edge analytics transform traditional rigid assembly lines into flexible, self-optimizing manufacturing ecosystems.

## Technical definition
The strategic paradigm of industrial transformation centered on cyber-physical integration, decentralized autonomous operational systems, pervasive real-time data transparency, and end-to-end supply chain interoperability.

## Examples
An athletic footwear factory utilizes Industry 4.0 automated cells to manufacture custom-fitted running shoes, automatically reprogramming 3D knitting machines and sole-dispensing robots upon receiving consumer foot scans from retail stores.
