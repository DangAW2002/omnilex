## Easy explanation
A deductible is your personal contribution before an insurance policy kicks in: if your car suffers $1,200 in fender damage and your deductible is $500, you pay the first $500 to the repair shop, and the insurance company covers the remaining $700.

## Details
Insurers use deductibles to mitigate moral hazard, discouraging policyholders from filing frivolous claims for minor routine costs and encouraging loss prevention. Deductibles are either applied per claim event (common in auto and property insurance) or accrued cumulatively over an annual policy calendar (typical in health insurance plans). Once an annual deductible is met, the policyholder typically enters a coinsurance or copay phase until reaching the statutory out-of-pocket maximum. Plans with high deductibles (such as High Deductible Health Plans) qualify individuals to contribute pre-tax earnings to Health Savings Accounts.

## Technical definition
A contractual threshold of loss retention borne exclusively by the insured entity prior to the attachment and disbursement of indemnity payments by the underwriter.

## Examples
A homeowner with a $1,500 insurance deductible files an $8,000 roof damage claim following a storm; the insurer issues a settlement check for $6,500 after subtracting the required deductible.
