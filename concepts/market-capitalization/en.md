## Easy explanation
Market capitalization is like putting a price tag on an entire company: if a company has issued 1 million shares and each share sells for $100 today, buying the whole company would cost $100 million.

## Details
Market capitalization reflects public consensus on a company's total equity value. Financial markets classify enterprises into broad categories: large-cap ($10 billion or more), mid-cap ($2 billion to $10 billion), and small-cap ($250 million to $2 billion), with micro-cap below that threshold. Large-cap businesses typically offer greater operational stability and dividend consistency, whereas small-cap firms possess faster growth runway but carry higher bankruptcy and liquidity risks. Major market indexes like the S&P 500 weight their components by free-float adjusted market cap rather than total shares to account for restricted insider holdings.

## Technical definition
The total equity market valuation of an incorporated entity determined by taking the aggregate number of all issued and outstanding common shares multiplied by the prevailing market trading price per share.

## Examples
If a technology corporation has 15 billion diluted common shares outstanding and each share trades at $200 on NASDAQ, the company's total market capitalization equals $3 trillion, categorizing it as a mega-cap market leader.
