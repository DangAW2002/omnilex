## Easy explanation
A mortgage is like financing a home through a long-term partnership with a lender: the bank pays the seller most of the purchase price upfront, you move in and make monthly payments over 15 to 30 years, and the bank holds a legal claim on the title until you finish paying off the loan.

## Details
Mortgages typically require an upfront down payment (commonly 3% to 20% of the purchase price) and are repaid through an amortization schedule where monthly installments cover principal, interest, property taxes, and homeowners insurance. Common loan structures include 15-year or 30-year fixed-rate mortgages, where the interest rate stays constant, and adjustable-rate mortgages (ARMs), where rates reset periodically based on benchmark financial indices. Because real estate secures the debt, the lender retains a statutory lien and can initiate foreclosure proceedings if the borrower defaults.

## Technical definition
A legal agreement and security instrument conveying an interest in real property to a creditor as collateral security for the performance of a monetary obligation, extinguishable upon complete satisfaction of the underlying promissory note.

## Examples
A homebuyer secures a $300,000, 30-year fixed-rate mortgage at 6.5%, resulting in a monthly principal and interest payment of $1,896, paying down principal progressively as the loan amortizes.
