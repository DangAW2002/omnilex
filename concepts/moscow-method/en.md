## Easy explanation
Think of MoSCoW as packing luggage under an airline weight restriction: shoes and passport are Musts (non-negotiable), a warm sweater is a Should, sunglasses are a Could, and heavy snorkeling fins are Won'ts left behind for this journey.

## Details
Originating in the Dynamic Systems Development Method (DSDM), MoSCoW categorizes deliverables based on necessity:
- Must have: Vital, non-negotiable requirements without which the release cannot launch legally or functionally.
- Should have: High-value features that are essential over the long run, but workarounds exist if temporarily omitted.
- Could have: Desirable enhancements that will only be tackled if surplus capacity remains after core duties are met.
- Won't have (this time): Agreed out-of-scope capabilities formally deferred to future release horizons.
Best practices recommend dedicating approximately 60% of capacity to Musts, leaving headroom for unexpected obstacles.

## Technical definition
A qualitative agile requirements triage framework classifying deliverables into four distinct tiers to establish clear operational boundaries under fixed deadlines.

## Examples
An online grocery store launch classifies checkout payment processing as Must-have, coupon redemption as Should-have, dark mode as Could-have, and cryptocurrency checkout as Won't-have.
