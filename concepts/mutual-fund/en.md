## Easy explanation
Think of a mutual fund as chartering a bus with hundreds of travelers: everyone chips in for the fuel and driver, and you travel together to a shared destination instead of driving your own car alone through heavy market traffic.

## Details
Investors purchase shares directly from the fund company or through licensed brokers. Rather than buying individual corporate equities or municipal bonds, an individual buys shares in the fund itself, which represents fractional ownership of the underlying assets. Mutual fund transactions do not occur in real time; trades are settled once daily after the market closes, based on the newly calculated Net Asset Value (NAV). Most mutual funds distribute dividends and capital gains annually, which investors can either receive in cash or automatically reinvest into additional shares. Management fees and operational expenses are deducted from NAV before returns are disbursed.

## Technical definition
An open-end management investment company registered under securities regulations that issues redeemable securities backed by a diversified portfolio of equities, debt instruments, or money market securities.

## Examples
An individual invests $5,000 in a balanced mutual fund holding 60% equities and 40% corporate bonds. The professional manager rebalances the holdings periodically, and at year-end, the fund distributes accrued dividend income and realized capital gains directly to the shareholder.
