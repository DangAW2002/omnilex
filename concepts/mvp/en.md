## Easy explanation
Think of an MVP as building a basic skateboard instead of waiting three years to manufacture a luxury sports car: both solve the fundamental challenge of getting from point A to point B, allowing you to test if people actually want personal mobility before investing millions in leather upholstery.

## Details
An MVP is not a half-baked, broken release; it is a carefully focused product that delivers genuine value on a single core promise. The goal is accelerating learning loops through build-measure-learn feedback cycles. By placing a functional solution in front of early adopters, product managers observe real behaviors, identify usability flaws, and confirm whether prospective buyers will pay, rather than relying on hypothetical survey responses. Feedback gathered directly dictates future backlog investments.

## Technical definition
The smallest release of a new product that still lets a team test its key assumptions with real customers, so it learns as much as possible for as little build effort as possible.

## Examples
The founders of a food delivery service test their concept by manually buying meals at local stalls and delivering them on bicycles via simple messaging apps before commissioning custom logistics software.
