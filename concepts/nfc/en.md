## Easy explanation
Think of a physical high-five between two smart devices: when you tap a smartphone against a payment terminal, electricity leaps across the magnetic gap to confirm your intentional transaction securely.

## Details
Near-Field Communication (NFC) evolved from 13.56 MHz High-Frequency RFID standards, deliberately limiting its operating radius to less than 4 centimeters. This physical proximity acts as an implicit security barrier, preventing casual interception or accidental triggering. NFC devices operate in three distinct modes: Card Emulation (allowing smartphones to behave like contactless payment cards), Reader/Writer (reading smart posters or hotel door keys), and Peer-to-Peer (swapping digital business cards or Wi-Fi pairing credentials between phones).

## Technical definition
A standards-based, short-range wireless communication protocol utilizing magnetic inductive coupling at a carrier frequency of 13.56 MHz with transmission rates up to 424 kbps across distances under four centimeters.

## Examples
A subway commuter taps an NFC-enabled smartphone against a turnstile reader; the phone emulates a contactless transit card and verifies the biometric token in less than 300 milliseconds.
