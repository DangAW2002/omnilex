## Easy explanation
Like grading a delivery truck driver by multiplying three factors: how many hours the truck was actually moving instead of broken down, how close they drove to the speed limit instead of crawling in traffic, and what percentage of packages arrived intact without being damaged.

## Details
Standardized under ISO 22400-2, Overall Equipment Effectiveness (OEE) calculates operational efficiency by multiplying three distinct percentages: Availability, Performance, and Quality. Availability measures uptime divided by planned production time, capturing losses from equipment breakdowns and setup changeovers. Performance calculates actual operating speed against theoretical ideal cycle time, highlighting micro-stops and reduced operating speeds. Quality reflects good parts produced divided by total parts started, capturing defects and startup scrap. An OEE score of 100% represents a perfect manufacturing process with zero downtime, maximum operating speed, and zero defects.

## Technical definition
A standardized quantitative operational performance indicator defined as the product of Availability, Performance rate, and Quality rate, reflecting total productive manufacturing output relative to planned capacity.

## Examples
A CNC milling cell planned for 8 hours (480 minutes) runs for 432 minutes (90% Availability), produces parts at 85% of theoretical speed (85% Performance), with 98% passing quality inspection (98% Quality), yielding an OEE of 74.97% (0.90 x 0.85 x 0.98).
