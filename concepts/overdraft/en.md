## Easy explanation
An overdraft is like a store clerk spotting you five dollars when your grocery bill exceeds the cash in your wallet, saving you from embarrassment at checkout but charging you a fee for the temporary loan.

## Details
When a bank customer attempts a purchase, check payment, or electronic transfer exceeding their available deposit balance, the bank can either decline the transaction for non-sufficient funds (NSF) or honor it under an overdraft program. Honoring the transaction creates a negative account balance and usually incurs an overdraft fee (frequently $30 to $35 per item). Many institutions offer overdraft protection transfers, linking the checking account to a savings account, secondary line of credit, or credit card to cover deficits automatically at lower costs.

## Technical definition
A short-term, uncollateralized credit extension arising when an authorized payment order or electronic withdrawal is executed against a deposit account with insufficient ledger funds.

## Examples
A customer with $40 in his checking account swipes his debit card for a $55 textbook; the bank clears the transaction, driving his balance to -$15 and assessing a $34 overdraft fee, requiring a $49 deposit to restore the account to zero.
