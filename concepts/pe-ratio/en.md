## Easy explanation
The P/E ratio is like the purchase price on an apartment building measured against its annual rental income: if a building costs $1,000,000 and generates $50,000 in net rent per year, its price-to-earnings multiple is 20, meaning it takes 20 years of current rent to match the purchase price.

## Details
The ratio is calculated by dividing the market price per share by earnings per share (EPS). Trailing P/E uses historical audited earnings from the preceding twelve months (TTM), whereas forward P/E relies on consensus analyst forecasts for the upcoming fiscal year. A high P/E multiple often signals that investors anticipate substantial future earnings expansion, while a low multiple can indicate an undervalued bargain or a business facing structural decline. Investors evaluate P/E by benchmarking against industry peers and historical five-year averages.

## Technical definition
A financial market multiple computed as the ratio of an equity security's current market capitalization or per-share price to its net operating income after tax on a diluted per-share basis.

## Examples
A company trading at $60 per share reports diluted net earnings of $3.00 per share over the past four quarters; its trailing P/E ratio is 20.0x.
