## Easy explanation
The policy rate is the baseline interest rate fixed by a nation’s central bank, dictating how expensive it is for regular banks to borrow money.

## Details
When central bankers raise the policy rate, commercial banks pass higher interest charges onto customer loans and mortgages, which cools overheated consumer spending and suppresses inflation. Lowering the rate makes borrowing cheap, encouraging businesses to invest and consumers to buy homes during economic downturns.

## Technical definition
The target key interest rate set by monetary policy committees governing short-term interbank liquidity operations and sovereign refinancing facilities.

## Examples
The Bank of England Monetary Policy Committee votes to increase Bank Rate by 25 basis points to 4.50 percent to temper persistent service sector price increases.
