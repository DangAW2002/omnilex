## Easy explanation
Predictive maintenance is like a machine having a continuous health checkup: instead of waiting for a factory motor to smoke and catch fire, or replacing expensive parts on a rigid calendar schedule when they are still perfectly fine, sensors detect microscopic vibrations and schedule a fix just in time.

## Details
Predictive maintenance (PdM) leverages high-frequency sensor measurements—including vibration spectrums, acoustic emissions, thermographic imagery, and lubricating oil dielectric properties. Condition monitoring algorithms analyze operational baseline drifts, applying statistical anomaly detection and machine learning models to forecast Remaining Useful Life (RUL). By intervening precisely when micro-flaws emerge, facilities avoid catastrophic unplanned factory downtime, minimize unnecessary component swaps, and optimize maintenance technician labor.

## Technical definition
A condition-driven maintenance paradigm that evaluates asset degradation via real-time telemetry analytics to determine the optimal timing for servicing prior to functional failure.

## Examples
A cement factory mounting triaxial accelerometers on kiln blower motors, using automated vibration frequency peak analysis to detect roller bearing flaking three weeks before catastrophic seizure.
