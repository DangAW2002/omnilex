## Easy explanation
Think of a product backlog as a restaurant order board where kitchen tickets are constantly sorted: dishes needed immediately move to the top, while long-simmering recipe experiments remain lower down until staff have room on the stove.

## Details
The product backlog is dynamic and constantly expanding or contracting based on customer feedback, market discoveries, and technical architecture needs. Items positioned near the peak undergo refinement: developers estimate complexity, clarify functional acceptance parameters, and break large ideas into discrete user stories. Deeper items remain broad concepts. The product owner guards this single source of truth, balancing immediate customer delight against long-term architectural stability.

## Technical definition
An emergent, ordered inventory of all functional and non-functional requirements, enhancements, and defect corrections maintained as the sole authorized source of work for a Scrum team.

## Examples
A digital bank maintains backlog items ranging from critical compliance updates ("Implement biometric transfer limits above 10 million VND") to aspirational UI changes ("Add animated celebration upon reaching savings goal").
