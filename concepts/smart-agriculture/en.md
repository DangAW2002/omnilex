## Easy explanation
Like equipping a farmer with digital magnifying glasses across every corner of the field, automatically turning on localized sprinklers and fertilizer sprayers only where plants are thirsty or hungry.

## Details
Formally outlined in ITU-T Recommendation Y.4450, smart agriculture replaces uniform field-wide treatments with micro-targeted, data-driven interventions. Battery-powered sensor nodes deployed throughout cropland measure soil electrical conductivity, volumetric water content, leaf wetness, and ambient solar radiation. Drones equipped with multispectral cameras capture NDVI imagery to evaluate canopy photosynthesis. These inputs drive automated variable-rate drip irrigation solenoids, fertilizer injection injectors, and robotic harvesters, optimizing water and chemical usage while increasing crop tonnage per hectare.

## Technical definition
The integrated management framework utilizing information and communication technologies, cyber-physical field sensors, and autonomous agricultural machinery to achieve high-efficiency, sustainable precision agricultural production.

## Examples
A commercial vineyard places soil moisture and microclimate probes across forty acres of terraced hillside, utilizing automated LoRaWAN drip valves to deliver custom water volumes to individual grape varietals based on daily evapotranspiration rates.
