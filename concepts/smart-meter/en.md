## Easy explanation
Think of the traditional spinning electricity dial on your home wall replaced by a connected computer: instead of a worker walking from house to house with a clipboard each month, the meter texts its exact power measurements directly to the utility company every hour.

## Details
Smart meters form the cornerstone of Advanced Metering Infrastructure (AMI) for electrical grids, water supplies, and natural gas systems. Unlike electro-mechanical induction meters, smart meters use precision analog-to-digital current transformers and voltage dividers to log consumption at frequent intervals (such as every 15 minutes). Equipped with cellular, power-line communication (PLC), or RF mesh networking modems, they transmit consumption records, power-outage alerts, and power-quality telemetry to billing centers, enabling time-of-use pricing and remote service actuation.

## Technical definition
An electronic solid-state utility metering instrument configured to measure and store energy or resource consumption data at high-frequency time intervals, featuring two-way telemetry interfaces for remote automated infrastructure integration.

## Examples
An electric utility smart meter records a household electricity draw every 15 minutes, transmitting consumption records over a 915 MHz RF mesh network to automate monthly billing without home visits.
