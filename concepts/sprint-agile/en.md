## Easy explanation
Picture a sprint as a short relay leg where runners direct all energy toward reaching a tangible flag within a fixed window before catching their breath and planning the next stretch.

## Details
Sprints typically run for two weeks, though teams may choose any duration up to a calendar month. The duration remains locked across cycles to establish a steady operational cadence. During a sprint, quality commitments cannot be diluted, team composition stays stable, and external parties cannot introduce disruptive scope modifications that threaten the overarching sprint goal. A succeeding sprint begins immediately once the active one finishes.

## Technical definition
The core cyclical timebox within Scrum that houses sprint planning, daily scrums, development implementation, sprint review, and retrospective activities.

## Examples
A software team operates on consecutive two-week sprints, using sprint 14 to build and test customer multi-factor login before demonstrating it to stakeholders.
