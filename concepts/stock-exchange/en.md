## Easy explanation
A stock exchange is like a central trading pavilion for financial investments: instead of consumer merchandise, traders gather under strict market rules to buy and sell slices of corporations at transparent, continuously updated auction prices.

## Details
Modern stock exchanges operate electronic central limit order books that automatically match buy orders (bids) with sell orders (asks) based on price-time priority. Exchanges impose rigorous listing standards on corporations, demanding regular audited financial reports, corporate governance compliance, and minimum public float thresholds. Major global exchanges include the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE), Nasdaq, London Stock Exchange (LSE), and Tokyo Stock Exchange (TSE). Exchanges also provide real-time price discovery, market surveillance against manipulation, and trade clearing through central counterparty clearinghouses.

## Technical definition
A formally organized and supervised secondary market facility providing auction mechanisms, trade execution infrastructure, and regulatory oversight for the standardized transfer of listed financial securities.

## Examples
A brokerage client places an electronic limit order to purchase 50 shares of an enterprise at $180; the stock exchange matching engine pairs the order against a seller's ask within microseconds and confirms execution.
