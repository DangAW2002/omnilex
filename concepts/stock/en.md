## Easy explanation
Owning stock is like buying a single slice of a giant commercial pizza: when the pizzeria expands and earns bigger profits, your slice becomes more valuable, but if business collapses, your slice shrinks in value.

## Details
Corporations issue shares of common and preferred stock to raise investment capital without taking on loan obligations. Stockholders participate in corporate growth through two primary channels: capital appreciation when the share price increases, and dividend distributions when the board of directors shares quarterly earnings. Common shareholders typically hold voting rights at annual general meetings to elect directors. However, in the event of corporate liquidation or bankruptcy, equity holders maintain residual claims and are paid last, only after bondholders and trade creditors have been satisfied.

## Technical definition
A financial instrument representing residual equity ownership in the capital structure of a joint-stock enterprise, conveying proportional rights to operating earnings and net liquidation assets.

## Examples
An investor purchases 100 shares of an enterprise at $50 per share; if the share price rises to $65 over two years while paying a $2 annual dividend per share, the total return equals $1,900.
