## Easy explanation
A term deposit is like locking your money inside a time-release vault for six months or a year: because you promise not to touch the cash until the timer expires, the bank rewards your commitment with a higher payout.

## Details
When placing funds into a term deposit (often called a Certificate of Deposit or CD), the depositor commits to leaving the principal untouched for durations ranging from one month to five years. In exchange, the financial institution guarantees a fixed interest rate unaffected by subsequent market fluctuations. If the depositor needs to withdraw the principal before the maturity date, the bank assesses an early withdrawal penalty, typically forfeiting several months of accrued interest. Upon maturity, the investor can either cash out or roll over into a new term deposit.

## Technical definition
A promissory time deposit liability of a financial institution paying a fixed or indexed coupon until a designated maturity date, governed by legal restrictions against premature liquidation without forfeiture of yield.

## Examples
An investor places $20,000 into a 12-month term deposit paying a fixed 5.0% APY, earning exactly $1,000 in guaranteed interest at maturity without exposure to market fluctuations.
