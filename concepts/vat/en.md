## Easy explanation
VAT is a consumption tax added onto products as they move through manufacturing and distribution, ultimately paid by the end customer when buying in store.

## Details
Unlike simple turnover taxes that compound repeatedly, registered businesses only remit tax on the value they personally create. Companies collect output tax from buyers, deduct the input tax already paid to their own suppliers, and remit the net difference to national tax authorities.

## Technical definition
An indirect, broad-based consumption tax collected fractionally through registered businesses, designed with an invoice-credit mechanism to avoid compounding.

## Examples
A furniture maker buys lumber for $100 plus $10 VAT, constructs a dining table sold for $250 plus $25 VAT, and forwards $15 net VAT to the revenue agency.
