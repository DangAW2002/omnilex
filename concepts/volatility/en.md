## Easy explanation
Volatility is like turbulence during a flight: a smooth flight glides steadily with barely a bump, while severe turbulence causes the plane to drop and bounce violently without changing where you are heading.

## Details
In finance, volatility quantifies how widely price swings deviate from the average return over time. It is commonly calculated as the annualized standard deviation of daily logarithmic price changes. Higher volatility indicates greater price uncertainty and wider swings in value, translating into elevated investment risk along with higher profit potential for short-term traders. Markets measure forward-looking volatility expectations through specialized indicators like the VIX index, which tracks option pricing on benchmark equity indexes. Long-term investors often use diversification and asset allocation to dampen portfolio volatility.

## Technical definition
A statistical dispersion metric representing the annualized standard deviation of the continuously compounded rate of return on a financial instrument.

## Examples
A steady public water utility stock trades within a tight range of $48 to $52 over an entire fiscal year, exhibiting low volatility, whereas a speculative early-stage biotech stock swings violently between $15 and $95 across several months, displaying high volatility.
